# {{Product}} — the market

**Why this file exists:** *"is this worth building"* gets answered from memory otherwise, and a
market number invented once is quoted for a year. **Every figure here carries where it came from
and when.** The guard refuses a figure without both — and `unknown` is an accepted answer, because
this file's job is not to have numbers, it is to make the numbers it has checkable.

**Three sizes, and they answer different questions.** Confusing them is how a plan that looked
funded turns out not to be:

| | Question it answers | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | if everyone who could ever want this bought it, how big is that? | it is a ceiling, not a target; nobody has ever sold to a TAM |
| **SAM** | how much of that can this product actually serve — this geography, this segment, this language, this price? | quietly re-derived from TAM by multiplying by a number somebody liked |
| **SOM** | how much can *we* reach in the period we are planning for, with the reach we actually have? | the one that decides anything, and the one most often skipped |

**Each figure, one line, with its provenance:**

- **TAM**: {{$X}} · source: {{who counted it, and how}} · {{YYYY-MM-DD}}
- **SAM**: {{$X or N units}} · source: {{who counted it, and how}} · {{YYYY-MM-DD}}
- **SOM**: {{$X or N units}} · source: {{who counted it, and how}} · {{YYYY-MM-DD}}

**Where a figure was derived rather than found, the derivation IS the source** — write the
arithmetic, not the result: *"SAM: 180,000 firms · source: national register SIC 10.71, filtered
to firms with 2+ sites · 2026-08-01"*. A derived number whose working is hidden cannot be argued
with, and it inherits every error in its inputs silently.
